Case 01 · Digital economy · Eurostat

Digital Europe

Do wealthier EU countries adopt more AI, cloud and digital skills? Five official Eurostat datasets, cleaned and combined in DuckDB, then tested with correlation analysis across all 27 member states.

01 / the question

What does digitalisation look like by economy?

Organisations investing in digital transformation need to know how technology adoption differs between markets and how that relates to economic conditions. The analysis focuses on association rather than causation, to identify patterns worth investigating further.

01 · ECONOMIC

GDP per capita in purchasing power standards (PPS) per inhabitant.

02 · ENTERPRISE TECH

Enterprise adoption of artificial intelligence and paid cloud-computing services.

03 · DIGITAL CAPABILITY

Individuals with at least basic digital skills, and ICT specialists as a share of employment.

02 / method

Raw data in. Trusted tables out.

Data cleaning

Raw Eurostat TSV files are preserved separately. Variables were filtered to the EU-27 population and converted into structured CSV datasets.

Quality control

Missing values and Eurostat status flags were checked before the analytical datasets were produced. The final 2023 and 2025 indicators have no missing analytical values.

Derived metrics

2025 values, 2023 to 2025 changes and Pearson correlations were calculated from the cleaned data.

04 / findings

What the analysis shows

FINDING 01

AI adoption increased substantially

Enterprise AI adoption rose by an average of 12.49 percentage points between 2023 and 2025 across the 27 countries.

FINDING 02

Cloud adoption also expanded

Enterprise cloud adoption rose by an average of 5.72 percentage points, with large differences between countries.

FINDING 03

Digital capability changed unevenly

Digital-skills prevalence rose by an average of 3.40 percentage points, with country changes differing substantially.

FINDING 04

Wealth and tech move together

GDP per capita correlated positively with all four indicators in 2025. This is association, not causation.
