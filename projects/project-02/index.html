Project 02 · Customer & Operational Analytics

Understanding Customer Purchasing Behavior in Online Retail

An analytical study of purchasing behavior, repeat customers, geography, product performance and cancellations using the UCI Online Retail II transaction dataset.

Business Question

How do customer purchasing behavior, repeat purchasing, geography and cancellations relate to revenue performance in an online retail business?

Project Overview

This project examines transaction-level retail data to understand how customer behavior and operational activity relate to revenue. The analysis moves from transaction-level data into customer, country, product and monthly performance views.

Rather than treating all transactions equally, the analysis distinguishes sales from cancellations and other transaction types. This makes it possible to examine both revenue generation and operational patterns that may affect reported sales.

Dataset

The project uses the Online Retail II dataset from the UCI Machine Learning Repository. The dataset contains transaction-level information from a UK-based registered non-store online retailer and covers December 2009 through December 2011.

1.07M+ Transaction records in the original workbook
2 Years Transaction period covered by the dataset
8 Original dataset columns
43 Countries represented in the analysis dataset

Analytical Questions

01 — Revenue Performance

How does revenue change over time, and which periods generate the highest observed sales?

02 — Customer Concentration

How is revenue distributed across customers, and how concentrated is revenue among high-value customers?

03 — Repeat Purchasing

How do repeat customers differ from one-time customers in purchasing frequency and revenue contribution?

04 — Geography

How does revenue vary across countries, and how does customer activity differ geographically?

05 — Product Performance

Which products contribute the most to observed sales revenue and sales volume?

06 — Cancellations

When do cancellations occur, and how does cancellation activity compare with sales activity?

Data Preparation

The raw workbook was processed into analytical datasets while preserving the original source data separately. Transaction records were standardized, numeric and date fields were validated, and transaction types were explicitly classified.

1
Load & Combine Read the two source worksheets and combine them into a single analytical transaction dataset.
2
Validate & Standardize Standardize column names and validate dates, quantities, prices, customer identifiers and text fields.
3
Classify Transactions Separate sales, cancellations, negative-quantity records, zero-price records and other transaction types.
4
Create Analytical Datasets Produce cleaned transactions, sales, cancellations and customer-level analytical datasets.
5
Analyze & Visualize Examine revenue, customers, countries, products and cancellations using SQL, Python and visual analysis.

Key Findings

  • The analysis contains 5,878 customers with valid customer identifiers available for customer-level analysis.
  • 4,255 customers were classified as repeat customers, while 1,623 were classified as one-time customers.
  • Repeat customers represented approximately 96.8% of analyzed customer revenue, compared with approximately 3.2% from one-time customers.
  • Revenue was highly concentrated among a relatively small group of customers. The top 10% of customers accounted for approximately 64.0% of customer revenue.
  • Customers with more frequent purchases generally generated more revenue. The relationship between invoice frequency and revenue was positive in the analyzed customer dataset.
  • Monthly revenue varied substantially over the observation period, with November 2011 recording the highest observed monthly sales revenue at approximately £1.50 million.

These findings describe observed relationships in the dataset. They should not be interpreted as evidence that purchasing frequency or customer characteristics directly caused higher revenue.
